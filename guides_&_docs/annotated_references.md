# The following are the resources that have helped me understand both the food101 dataset and how transfer learning works. 

## Pre-Requisite Knowledge:
Basic knowledge about how neural networks work and some understanding of sk-learn. 

### Recommended Textbook:
1. Practical Machine Learning for Computer Vision End-to-End Machine Learning for Images by Valliappa Lakshmanan, Martin Görner, and Ryan Gillard.
    Annotation: This is the core textbook that I used to understand CNN and Transfer Learning. I recommend reading the first three chapters of this book, as it contains many useful information, code examples, and background knowledge. I believe that reading the first three chapters of this textbook will give necessary prerequisite knowledge to start reading research papers and understand the field of Image Classification. 

### Recommended Websites: 
1. GeeksforGeeks
    link: https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/
    Annotation: This is the go to websites for Machine Learning and Data Science Information. 

2. PapersWithCode
    link: https://paperswithcode.co/benchmark/food-101
    Annotation: This is a good website to regularly check. It contains information about papers, models, benchmarks, etc.


### Recommended Youtube Videos: 
1. Transfer learning - explained (VGG16, MobileNet, ResNet, EfficientNet) by TileStats
    link: https://youtu.be/YaBX-eNdYGw?si=f5FXOWFIpucjPuIy
    Annotation: This video explains how transfer learning works in a very simple step by step manner with visuals. This video also includes step by step python codes that will help in learning the basics of transfer learning with tensorflow/keras in python. The channel also has great videos on introductory neural network concepts and convolutional neural network concepts. 

2. Food Image Classification (Transfer Learning) - Data Every Day #202 by Gabriel Atkin
    link: https://youtu.be/35iRdqY01co?si=ix5-yLIfgbc3Y5qn
    Annotation: This video demonstrates step by step transfer learning workflow for food101 dataset. A very good reference. The preprocessing step used here is different from my code and I want to recommend finding the differences in terms of performance & requirements for further understanding.

3. But what is a convolution? by 3Blue1Brown
    link: https://youtu.be/KuXjwB4LzSA?si=k95Rvp4jzN9T0tkP
    Annotation: To understand the concept of convolution that underlies Convolutional Neural Network, I want to recommend this video. Grant (Author of 3Blue1Brown) makes really educational videos that's great for data science students. His playlists like Linear Algebra, Neural Networks Series, and Calculus have helped me alot too. 

### Recommended Papers: 
1. Jiang, M. (2019). Food image classification with convolutional neural networks. Department of Computer Science, Stanford University. https://cs230.stanford.edu/projects_fall_2019/reports/26233496.pdf
    Annotation: This along with the the paper below are two papers that I believe are student papers with course/assessment guidelines that are similar to ours. It is good to see how different students are working on image classification for food 101 dataset.

2. Gjertsen, K. (2020). Food classification with CNNs. Department of Symbolic Systems, Stanford University. https://cs230.stanford.edu/projects_fall_2020/reports/55825128.pdf

3. Bossard, L., Guillaumin, M., & Van Gool, L. (2014, September). Food-101–mining discriminative components with random forests. In European conference on computer vision (pp. 446-461). Cham: Springer International Publishing.
    Annotation: This is the original paper for the food 101 dataset. I recommend reading section 3 that talks about the dataset.

4. Huang, G., Liu, Z., Van Der Maaten, L., & Weinberger, K. Q. (2017). Densely connected convolutional networks. In Proceedings of the IEEE conference on computer vision and pattern recognition (pp. 4700-4708).
    Annotation: This is the paper for DenseNet that I have used.
